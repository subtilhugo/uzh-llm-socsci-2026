You code one whole French legislative candidate manifesto. For each of the 12 themes below, say whether the manifesto substantively contains it anywhere. Code each theme independently; a manifesto may have several. The French text is authoritative. The text may contain OCR errors: read through them, but do not guess content that cannot be recovered. When a theme is present, give one verbatim French quote of at most 15 words as evidence; when it is absent, give an empty quote.

local_offer: a geographically specific local issue, institution, project, service, firm, infrastructure, place, or population. A candidate name, constituency heading, or place name alone is insufficient.
national_offer: substantive national policy, national institutions, government, the presidency, national economic policy, or a nationwide political objective. A generic mention of France is insufficient. A bare election or government-formation appeal is insufficient, but electoral framing does not erase substantive national content. Generic calls to save France, change, win, defeat a government, or restore hope require a substantive national policy, institution, issue, programme, or objective.
redistribution: allocation or reallocation of economic resources through a transfer, benefit, subsidy, progressive, wealth, or capital tax, tax credit, direct financial aid, social transfer, or comparable mechanism. Merely targeting or benefiting a group is insufficient. Do not automatically code generic purchasing power, prosperity, ordinary wage or SMIC changes, wage freezes or indexation, generic tax cuts, VAT reduction, employment creation, targeted public services, sectoral support, or preservation of producer income without an identifiable resource mechanism.
social_protection: pensions, social insurance, benefits, reimbursement or entitlement, unemployment insurance, minimum-income schemes, family support, disability or social-risk protection, and analogous protections. For health, insurance, reimbursement, entitlement, and social-risk protection belong here; hospitals, beds, doctors, facilities, staff, and service capacity belong under public_services.
employment: substantive discussion of jobs, unemployment, hiring, employment creation, redundancies or dismissals, job loss, employment outcomes, and employment policy. Working conditions may count when substantively connected to employment or labour conditions. Pure remuneration or wage policy, including SMIC or salary increases, wage freezes, wage indexation, and wage negotiations, does not count unless linked to employment, hiring, unemployment, job retention, job creation, or job loss.
industry: substantive discussion of manufacturing, factories, mines, production, industrial sectors, industrial firms, or industrial policy. A stray firm or factory word is insufficient.
deindustrialisation: only loss or contraction: plant or factory closure, redundancy, relocation, industrial decline, mine closure, industrial-job loss, or reconversion. An industry or factory mention alone is insufficient.
public_services: substantive discussion of publicly provided services such as schools, hospitals, post offices, railways, courts, administrations, policing, or local public institutions. Substantive rural or public mobility provision, including everyday trains, may count.
public_service_withdrawal: closure, disappearance, reduced access, centralisation, deterioration, or perceived abandonment of public services. Reopen, restore, re-establish, bring back, reconstruct, or reverse closure may establish prior loss. Strengthen, modernise, redeploy, improve, or increase alone does not. A medical desert indicates territorial access disadvantage but does not automatically establish withdrawal.
territorial_inequality: unequal treatment, opportunities, resources, or access between territories, explicit or clearly implied. A local complaint alone is insufficient. Geographically structured segregation and unequal opportunity can qualify.
class_divisive: social or economic groups framed in conflict or opposition, or one socioeconomic group portrayed as benefiting while another loses. Partisan conflict, anti-government or anti-party rhetoric, generic criticism of markets or ultraliberalism, and simple mentions of workers, employers, rich, poor, or elites are insufficient.
class_unifying: substantive common interest, shared burdens or resources, cooperation, solidarity, or unity across social, economic, or political groups. Generic occurrences of ensemble, union, solidarite, rassemblement, union nationale, or en commun, and routine coalition or electoral endorsement, are insufficient.
